About

I study how long non-coding RNAs guide stem cells toward becoming cartilage, using bioinformatics to advance regenerative medicine, healthy aging, and space life sciences.

I’m a PhD candidate in Biomedicine at Universidad de los Andes in Santiago, Chile, working in the Laboratorio de Biología Molecular y Bioinformática with Gino Nardocci. My work combines experimental and computational approaches, from stem cell biology and transcriptomics to space biology, which I pursue as a member of NASA GeneLab’s Multiomics Analysis Working Group.

Before this, I studied Biotechnological Processes at Universidad San Francisco de Quito in Ecuador, where my undergraduate thesis used molecular modeling to study POU1F1 mutations in hypopituitarism. My doctoral training also took me to the Albert Einstein College of Medicine for a research internship. Outside the lab, I do science outreach: I edited and wrote for Revista CiiB, ran scientific workshops with high schoolers, and tutored math and English for children whose schooling was disrupted by the pandemic.

Research

My research asks which long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) steer mesenchymal stem cells toward becoming cartilage-producing chondrocytes. I’m currently focused on:

  • Long non-coding RNAs
  • Chondrogenesis
  • Stem cells and regenerative medicine
  • Transcriptomics and multi-omics
  • Space biology and aging
  • Mitochondrial biology

Current projects

Long non-coding RNAs in cartilage cell differentiation

Doctoral thesis, with Gino Nardocci

Identifying the lncRNAs that are key to chondrocyte differentiation, as a step toward targeted modulation of stem cell phenotype. I’ve presented this work at EMBL, NYSCF, and the Chilean Society for Biochemistry and Molecular Biology.

  • Lab page

Aging and frailty in spaceflight

Ongoing since 2022, with the NASA GeneLab Multiomics Analysis Working Group

I analyze transcriptomic datasets on sarcopenia and frailty as part of international collaborations in aging and space biology. Our 2024 paper in Scientific Reports shows that aging and putative frailty biomarkers are altered by spaceflight.
